# Track D evidence

- **Candidate `frontier/d-claude-architecture-5`: exact degree-2 contraction, in place.** Vertices with out-degree <= 2 that are not query endpoints are bypassed while exactly one way leads on (dead ends dropped, branching vertices stay survivors, 64-step cap, exact 64-bit shortcut weights). The pass rewrites raw edges in place from a snapshot table of the bypassable vertices' edge words, so there is no second edge array; the escape table is merged afterwards. Base: PR 2 champion (`0c455ef` source).
- **What made contraction profitable (vs the neutral `-4` experiment):** the pass fell from 84-91 ms to 58.7 ms CPU at one thread (18.4 ms wall at four) by dropping the 20 MB `res` copy and its stores (in place), reading walk starts from a flat edge loop with branchless start recording and deferred root recovery, and using larger blocks. Measured attribution: the old scan was dominated by per-vertex loop-exit mispredicts and by 40-byte walk records written for every edge; the walks themselves were only 37 ms.
- **Paired local result (4 cores, 41 interleaved rounds vs PR 2):** median 0.245 vs 0.271 s (-9.6%), min 0.216 vs 0.245 s; fresh-cgroup peak at 12 threads with THP=always 74.8 vs 81.5 MB (-8%); formatted lines 855 vs 721. Local K estimate x1.052 (T) x1.017 (M) x0.975 (S) = about +4.3%. Not scoring-box data; the 4-core factors may not transfer.
- **Qualification:** 83 differential cases and 120-seed lattice plus contraction-shape stress at 1/4/12 workers; ASan/UBSan and TSan clean on both suites and sample data at 1/4/12 workers; 30 full-size and 150 sample repeat runs at 1/4/12 workers with zero mismatches; tiny/sample seed 2 byte-identical to the Python reference; sample 1 and full seed 1 SHAs match (full also at 12 threads). Pinned Docker run at 1/4/12 workers in CI.
- **Measured and not adopted this round:** (1) fusing the bypass flags and edge snapshot into the raw CSR build: no time gain and the 12-thread peak rose 74.8 to 91 MB because the 14 MB snapshot then coexists with the bins; (2) SSSE3/SSE4.1 parsing: a 16-byte load plus one shuffle converts `u` and `v` together and cuts parse CPU 116 to 98.5 ms (-15%) once the parse lambda carries the target attribute so it inlines, but a SIMD `w` on top is slower (105 ms, serialized behind the separator search); the pair-only variant is worth about 2% of T for +25 lines, a net of about +0.5% K.
- **Rollback:** PR 2 champion (K=23.855961).

Previous history follows.


- **Board result for the donation candidate (PR 3): rejected.** Luna's controlled A/B scored K=22.276 (T=0.148357 s, M=98.5 MB, S=779) against K=23.856 (T=0.132055 s, M=98.4 MB, S=728) for PR 2. The 12-thread balance model predicted a tie; it missed the cost of the idle-thread yield/spin loop (an SMT sibling's resources) and the mutex-protected donation queue. Lesson: the board's T tracks total CPU work divided by about 7.8 effective cores, not critical-path balance, so changes must be judged by CPU work.
- **Exact degree-2 contraction (branch `frontier/d-claude-architecture-4`, source blob `git hash-object track-d/d_traverse.cpp`): neutral, not proposed.** Vertices with out-degree <= 2 that are not query endpoints are bypassed by a unique-continuation walk (dead ends dropped, branching vertices stay survivors, 64-step cap forces a survivor); shortcut weights are exact 64-bit. Offline it removes 31% of vertices and 22% of edges, shortens solve CPU by 27-33%, and shrinks reverse/grow/gather. The price is a resolve pass over all 5M edges (110-125 ms CPU: 36 baseline plus 76 for walks) that nearly cancels the gain: total CPU -3% at 1 thread and flat at 4. Paired 4-core timing median -6.5% (0.288 vs 0.308 s over 41 rounds), but +131 formatted lines (852 vs 721; S about -2.4% K) and +3 MB (about -0.7% K), so the net K estimate is about zero. It would pay if the resolve pass cost under about 60 ms CPU.
- **Qualification of that branch:** 83 differential cases and 80+80-seed lattice and contraction-shape stress (long chains past the cap, one-way rings, diamonds, hubs, heavy edges) at 1/4/12 workers; ASan/UBSan and TSan clean; 144 repeat runs at 1/4/12 workers with zero mismatches; tiny/sample seed 2 byte-identical to the Python reference; sample 1 and full seed 1 SHAs match.
- **Dead ends measured this round:** 32-bit labels (slower, more instructions), forward probe length (identical instruction counts), push pruning by the other side's bound, prefetch of permutation entries, bucket-array size, query scheduling policies (ceiling about 2% under a 6-core SMT model), huge-page hints, and a private visited filter plus claim prefetch in the BFS (within noise).

With four threads, probes 64, 128, 256 and 512 all rounded to 0.38 s; keep 256. - **Gates:** 83 differential cases x 3 thread counts x 6 probe limits; tiny seed 2 and sample seed 2 byte-identical to the Python reference; sample seed 1 and full seed 1 SHA match the published values; ASan/UBSan clean; pinned Docker build and run match ([CI run](https://github.com/insightlabs38-pixel/sd-td/actions/runs/41148327539)).
- **Measured by phase (4 threads, ms):** parse+bin 30, raw CSR 15, BFS regions 35, assemble 8, gather 22, reverse 23, queries ~150. Single-thread query CPU ~0.6 s for 7.4M settles.
- **Findings:** random relabeling made every label access a cache miss; BFS renumbering alone cut query CPU 2.4x. A shared claim bitmap was fine, but adjacent per-region counters false-shared and cost 2x until moved to locals. Atomic CSR scatter cost 85 ms vs 15 ms for bucketed no-atomic build. Packed/Dial-style queues helped only once the bucket array was lean. Transparent huge pages gave a small median gain but a bad tail on this VM, so they were removed. A forward probe of 256 settles is required: pure bidirectional search floods the 1.7M-node main component for island-source queries.
